E4 — What the released RFT dataset says (zero compute)

Source: delinqu/comet-1.5k meta/episodes.jsonl + meta/info.json, 1469 episodes, 39 tasks. Ran before any GPU work, because the paper's post-training claim is the one we cannot afford to re-run — but the artifact it produced is public.

Hypothesis

The paper attributes the jump from Q=0.224 (challenge) to Q=0.345 (post-challenge) to a refined "task balancing" strategy applied when selecting 1469 trajectories out of ~25,500 rollouts (3 rounds × ~8500). If balancing is what mattered, the kept set should look more uniform across tasks than the raw success distribution.

Observations

1. Confirmed: 1469 episodes. Matches §4.3 exactly. Good provenance signal.

2. "Task balancing" is a cap, not a rebalance. Per-task episode counts:

120 120 120 120 120 118 111 109 65 58 50 44 37 32 31 30 26 24 14 12 11 11 10 9 8 8 8 7 7 6 6 4 4 2 2 2 1 1 1

Five tasks sit at exactly 120, with three more just under. That flat ceiling is the balancing — over-represented tasks are capped, rare tasks are not lifted. The result is still 120:1 skewed; the top 5 tasks hold 40.8% of the data, and the median task contributes 12 episodes.

3. RFT yield collapses with task horizon. Spearman ρ(episodes kept, mean episode length) = −0.45 across 39 tasks:

Kept episodes Tasks Mean horizon
≥ 100 8 7,457 frames (4.1 min)
10–99 15 16,563 frames (9.2 min)
< 10 16 16,212 frames (9.0 min)

Rejection sampling harvests almost entirely from the short half of the benchmark. This is the paper's own admission — "the sampling efficiency is still too low" — made quantitative. It also explains the shape of Fig 4: the tasks that survive post-training are the tasks RFT could feed.

4. Eleven of fifty tasks yielded nothing. 39 tasks appear; ~25,500 rollouts produced zero retained successes on the other 11. RFT is silent exactly where the benchmark is hardest, so it cannot by construction close the gap to the 0.611 "theoretical best" the paper reports.

5. Native resolution is 720 (head) / 480 (wrist) — exactly the "high resolution" row of Table 3. So that ablation's 0.30 → 0.60 gain is not from adding information; it is from ceasing to throw away the data's native fidelity by downsampling to 224. Reframes the paper's "high-fidelity perception is essential" conclusion as "don't downsample your data."

6. Minor integrity note. info.json reports total_frames: 12,226,350; summing episode lengths in episodes.jsonl gives 14,896,614 (Δ = 2.67 M, 18%). Likely a stale count not regenerated after final filtering. Flagged, not load-bearing.

Insight

The headline improvement is credited to "task balancing," but the released artifact shows balancing did comparatively little — the distribution stays extremely skewed. What the data actually shows is a horizon-dependent sampling bias: RFT works where episodes are short. That is a sharper and more actionable statement of the method's limitation than the paper makes, and it predicts where E1's per-task numbers should fall.
